The lighter copper and thinnest dielectric both help it wrap a 61 mm barrel. The surface finish is a required field but does nothing here, as no copper is exposed.

3M468 under Stiffener is worth taking. It is factory-applied double-sided adhesive, so the strips arrive ready to peel and wrap rather than leaving you to align a long strip of wet glue. The zip carries a Bottom Stiffener.GBR layer marking where it goes — selecting the option alone isn't enough, JLC need the area too.

If you order without the tape, delete that file first, or the layer and the order won't match and they will query it the other way.

Expect a DFM query. No exposed copper and an empty soldermask layer is unusual enough that their engineers may email first. It is a legend-only decorative flex with no electrical function: the copper pour is deliberate, and the empty mask layer is what leaves the coverlay covering the whole strip.

Check their Gerber preview before paying — the silkscreen layer should show the scale, and the outline should read 189.0 × 13.0 mm.

Printing the strip

  1. Print at 100%. Turn off “fit to page” and “scale to fit” — the file carries real millimetre dimensions and any scaling ruins the scale.
  2. Measure before you cut. The strip should be exactly 189.0 × 13.0 mm. If it isn't, something rescaled it.
  3. Laser on matte white adhesive label stock gives the crispest result. Inkjet will bleed on this much solid black and the tick edges go soft.
  4. Cut on the outline.
  5. Wrap it with the ∞ mark at your infinity index and work round from there.

It is a lot of solid black for a desktop printer. If yours struggles to lay it down evenly, the Gerber route gives a far better strip — and a more durable one.
